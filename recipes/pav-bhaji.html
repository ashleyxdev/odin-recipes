Pav Bhaji

Mumbai's Beloved Buttery Street Food, Served Hot Off the Tawa

Pav Bhaji is the soul of Mumbai's street food culture — a thick, spiced vegetable mash cooked on a flat iron griddle and served alongside soft butter-toasted bread rolls. Born in the 1850s as a quick meal for textile mill workers, it has since become one of India's most universally loved comfort foods, found everywhere from roadside stalls to restaurant menus.

The bhaji is a medley of mashed vegetables — potatoes, cauliflower, peas, capsicum, and tomatoes — simmered together with a signature blend of spices called Pav Bhaji Masala. The magic lies in the generous amount of butter worked into the bhaji as it cooks, giving it a rich, velvety finish with a deep red-orange hue. The pav rolls are sliced open, buttered, and toasted on the same tawa until golden and slightly crisp.

Served with finely chopped onion, a squeeze of lemon, and a cube of butter melting over the hot bhaji, it is a dish that is simultaneously humble and indulgent. Every household and every street vendor has their own version, but the warmth and satisfaction it delivers are always the same.

Ingredients:

  • 3 medium potatoes, boiled and mashed
  • 1 cup cauliflower florets, boiled
  • ½ cup green peas, boiled
  • 1 medium capsicum, finely chopped
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped (plus extra for serving)
  • 2 tsp Pav Bhaji Masala
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • Salt to taste
  • 4 tbsp butter (plus extra for toasting)
  • 1 tbsp oil
  • 8 pav bread rolls
  • Fresh coriander and lemon wedges for garnish

Steps:

  1. Heat oil and 2 tbsp butter in a wide pan or tawa over medium heat.
  2. Sauté onions until golden, then add capsicum and cook for 2 minutes.
  3. Add tomatoes and cook until they break down into a soft paste.
  4. Add all the boiled and mashed vegetables. Mix well and mash everything together using a potato masher.
  5. Stir in Pav Bhaji Masala, red chilli powder, turmeric, and salt. Add a splash of water if needed.
  6. Cook on medium heat for 8–10 minutes, mashing continuously. Add remaining butter and mix in.
  7. Slice pav rolls open, apply butter, and toast on a hot tawa until golden.
  8. Serve bhaji topped with a knob of butter, chopped onion, coriander, and a lemon wedge alongside the toasted pav.
