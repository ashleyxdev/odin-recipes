Paani Puri

Crispy Hollow Shells Filled with Spiced Potato and Dunked in Tangy Mint Water

Paani Puri is arguably the most universally adored street food in India, known by different names across regions — Golgappa in Delhi and Punjab, Puchka in West Bengal, and Gupchup in parts of Odisha and Bihar. At its core, it is a two-bite experience: a thin, crispy semolina shell is punctured open, filled with a spiced mixture of mashed potato and chickpeas, and then dunked into an intensely flavoured spiced water before being popped whole into the mouth.

The paani — the spiced water — is what defines a great Paani Puri. It is made by blending fresh mint and coriander with green chillies, tamarind, black salt, roasted cumin, and chaat masala, then chilling it thoroughly. The balance of tangy, spicy, salty, and herby in that single sip of water is what makes this dish so addictive. A sweeter tamarind chutney version of the water is often served alongside for contrast.

What makes Paani Puri truly special is the ritual of eating it — the puri must be filled and eaten in one swift motion before the crispy shell softens. At street stalls, the vendor fills each one to order and hands them out in rapid succession, and the challenge is to keep up. Making it at home brings that same joy, making it perfect for gatherings and casual celebrations alike.

Ingredients:

For the Puri (or use store-bought):

  • 1 cup fine semolina (sooji/rava)
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Water as needed to form a stiff dough
  • Oil for deep frying

For the Paani (Spiced Water):

  • 1 cup fresh mint leaves
  • ½ cup fresh coriander
  • 2–3 green chillies
  • 1 inch ginger
  • 3 tbsp tamarind pulp
  • 1 tsp roasted cumin powder
  • 1 tsp chaat masala
  • ½ tsp black salt (kala namak)
  • Salt to taste
  • 4 cups chilled water

For the Filling::

  • 3 medium potatoes, boiled and roughly mashed
  • ½ cup boiled chickpeas or white peas
  • 2–3 green chillies
  • 1 tsp chaat masala
  • ½ tsp red chilli powder
  • Salt to taste
  • Finely chopped onion and coriander (optional)

Steps:

  1. Make the dough: Mix semolina, flour, and salt. Add water gradually to form a stiff, smooth dough. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for 20 minutes.
  2. Roll and fry the puris: Roll the dough thin (2–3mm). Cut into small circles using a round cutter. Deep fry in medium-hot oil, pressing gently with a spoon to help them puff. Fry until golden and crisp. Drain and cool completely.
  3. Make the paani: Blend mint, coriander, chillies, and ginger with a little water into a smooth paste. Strain into a large bowl. Mix in tamarind pulp, cumin powder, chaat masala, black salt, and regular salt. Add 4 cups of chilled water, stir well, and taste-adjust. Refrigerate until serving.
  4. Prepare the filling: Mix mashed potatoes, chickpeas, chaat masala, red chilli powder, and salt. Add onion and coriander if using.
  5. Assemble: Gently crack a small hole in the top of each puri. Fill with a teaspoon of the potato-chickpea mixture.
  6. Dunk the filled puri fully into the chilled paani and eat immediately in one bite.
